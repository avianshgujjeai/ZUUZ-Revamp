A VP of Sales comparing Gong alternatives is usually not shopping for a feature list. The question underneath the search is sharper: where do our deals actually move, and which tool covers that surface without a contract that outgrows the team.

Most roundups answer that by comparing call recorders to each other. That is useful when the motion is call-heavy. It is incomplete when deals advance through email between calls, which is the reality for distribution, IT services, and most B2B teams running multi-thread accounts.

This guide describes Gong factually, lays out the real reasons teams evaluate alternatives, and groups the options by category so the choice maps to a motion rather than a brand. It also names the category most lists leave out: the email-signal and execution layer where ZUUZ sits.

What Gong Does and Does Well

Gong is a conversation and revenue intelligence platform built around call recording and transcription. It captures sales calls and meetings, transcribes them, and applies AI to surface coaching moments, deal risk, and conversation patterns across a team. On G2, Gong holds a 4.7 out of 5 rating across more than 6,600 reviews (2026) and is listed in the conversation intelligence and revenue intelligence categories.

The core strength is clear. When a sales motion runs through recorded calls and demos, Gong gives managers a searchable record of what was said, who said it, and how the conversation tracked against the deal. That supports coaching at scale and gives revenue leaders signal they would otherwise have to gather call by call.

Gong has also expanded beyond pure call analysis into forecasting and deal execution features over time. For teams whose pipeline lives on calls, it is a well-reviewed and widely adopted choice. The reasons teams still evaluate alternatives are not about whether Gong works. They are about cost, scope, and fit.

Why Sales Teams Evaluate Gong Alternatives

Three factual reasons drive most Gong alternative searches. None of them require claiming the product is weak. They are about whether the tool matches a given team's budget, motion, and stack.

Cost and Contract Structure

Gong uses custom enterprise pricing quoted per seat, typically with a platform fee, and does not publish a public self-serve price. That model fits larger organizations and prompts smaller teams to compare options with published or lower entry pricing. Cost is one of the most common terms paired with this search, and "gong cost" is a frequent follow-up query for a reason.

Call-Centric Scope

Gong's design centers on recorded calls and meetings. For teams whose deals progress mainly through email, shared inboxes, and forwarded threads, a call-first tool covers part of the motion and leaves the email surface to manual logging. That is a scope question, not a quality question.

Fit for Email-Driven Motions

Many B2B sales motions, especially in distribution and IT services, move between calls over email: renewal mentions, RFP attachments, pricing questions, and procurement back-and-forth. Teams running those motions often want a tool that captures email signals and updates the CRM, which is a different job from analyzing calls. As covered in how to fix missed sales leads in email, the deals that slip are usually the ones that never left the inbox.

These three reasons are not mutually exclusive. A mid-market IT services team might evaluate alternatives because the Gong quote outpaced the budget, and then realize during the evaluation that the bigger gap is the email surface their call tool never touched. That is why framing the search by category matters more than ranking products. The right comparison set depends on which of the three reasons is driving the decision, and a faulty CRM pipeline often points to the email surface rather than the call one.

Gong Alternatives by Category

The fastest way to shortlist is by category, because the categories solve different problems. A call recorder, a forecasting platform, an engagement tool, and an email-signal layer are not interchangeable. Below is a factual map of the main names in each.

Conversation Intelligence Peers

These tools do the same core job as Gong: record sales calls, transcribe them, and surface coaching and deal signals. They are the closest direct comparisons.

  • Avoma is an AI meeting assistant and conversation intelligence platform that records, transcribes, and summarizes calls, with scheduling and note-taking features. It is frequently positioned as a lower-cost option for smaller teams.
  • Jiminny is a conversation intelligence and call recording platform focused on coaching and revenue intelligence, commonly evaluated by mid-market sales teams.
  • Sybill is an AI assistant for sales that generates call summaries, behavioral insights, and follow-up emails from recorded conversations.
  • Chorus by ZoomInfo is a conversation intelligence platform that records and analyzes calls, now part of the ZoomInfo data platform.

Forecasting and Revenue Platforms

These tools sit above the CRM and focus on forecast accuracy and pipeline risk rather than call analysis. Teams evaluate them when the problem is predicting the number, not coaching the call.

  • Clari is a revenue platform centered on forecasting, pipeline management, and revenue operations workflows.
  • Aviso is an AI-based revenue operations and forecasting platform that scores deals and projects pipeline outcomes.

Sales Engagement

Engagement tools focus on outbound sequencing, cadences, and rep activity rather than recording conversations.

  • Salesloft is a sales engagement platform for cadences, email sequencing, and rep workflow, with conversation features added over time.

The Category Most Lists Skip: Email Signal and Execution

Almost every Gong alternative roundup compares call recorders to each other. That makes sense when the entire question is which tool best analyzes calls. It misses the teams whose deals do not live on calls in the first place.

In distribution and IT services, a single account can run a renewal, an active quote thread, and a new project scope through the same procurement contact at once. Those signals arrive as email: an RFP attachment in a shared inbox, a renewal mention in a support thread, a pricing question forwarded on a Friday afternoon. A call recorder, however good, does not see any of it.

The result is a CRM that reflects what reps remembered to log rather than what actually happened in accounts. As explored in why your CRM pipeline is wrong, that gap is not a reporting problem. It is a capture problem that reporting then inherits. An email-signal and execution layer fixes the source, which is why it belongs on any honest list of Gong alternatives for email-driven teams.

There is a second reason this category rarely appears on roundups: most of those lists are published by conversation intelligence vendors comparing themselves to a peer. A call recorder writing about Gong alternatives will name other call recorders. None of them solves the email problem, so none of them raises it. That is the gap this guide is meant to fill, and it maps directly to the broader revenue intelligence software stack rather than to the call layer alone.

Email Signal Versus Call Intelligence

The cleanest way to hold the distinction is by the question each tool answers. A conversation intelligence tool answers how a recorded call went and what to coach next. An email-signal layer answers which deals are moving in the inbox right now and whether the CRM knows about them. One looks backward at a conversation that was captured on purpose. The other looks at the live thread that no one thought to log. For a team running sales pipeline management software on top of stale records, the second question is usually the one costing revenue.

How to Choose the Right Gong Alternative

The choice gets simple once it is framed by motion rather than feature count. Name where deals actually move, then match the category to that surface.

Start With Where Deals Move

If most deal progress happens on calls and demos, a conversation intelligence tool is the right category: Gong, Avoma, Jiminny, Sybill, or Chorus by ZoomInfo. If the problem is forecast accuracy, look at Clari or Aviso. If the bottleneck is outbound sequencing, look at Salesloft. If deals advance over email and the CRM is chronically behind, the email-signal and execution category is the fit.

Compare on Cost per Active User and Contract Terms

Headline price is misleading. Compare total cost per active user, contract length, and which capabilities are included versus sold as add-ons. Gong's custom enterprise pricing means the real number depends on the quote, so any alternative should be compared on the same total-cost basis rather than on a sticker.

Check CRM Fit and Manual Work Removed

The last filter is operational. Does the tool work with the CRM already in place, and how much manual logging does it remove. According to the Salesforce State of Sales report (2024), sellers spend a large share of the workweek on tasks other than active selling, and CRM data entry is a recurring drain. A tool that keeps the CRM current without rep effort returns that time, which is the practical payoff behind every alternative on this list.
